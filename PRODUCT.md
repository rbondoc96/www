# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

A mixed professional audience landing on Rodrigo Bondoc's personal site:

- **Hiring managers / recruiters** who skim. They need to grasp seniority, what he
  builds, and credibility in seconds — Experience and Projects carry this weight.
- **Peer engineers and potential collaborators** who go deep. They read for taste,
  technical depth, and craft — the Blog (future) and Projects are where they linger.

The design must serve both reading speeds at once: fast credibility on the surface,
rewarding depth underneath. Visitors typically arrive from a link (a job application,
a GitHub profile, a referral) on desktop or mobile, give it a short first impression,
and decide whether to read on.

## Product Purpose

A personal site and portfolio for Rodrigo Bondoc, a software engineer in San
Francisco. It exists to present his work, experience, and writing under one
considered, on-brand surface — and to make the people evaluating him (to hire,
collaborate, or read) want to keep reading.

Surfaces: Home (a spare landing on the "RDB" monogram), Experience, Projects, and a
Blog. **The blog has no content yet but is a first-class design target** — long-form
reading (comfortable measure, clear hierarchy, restful typography) must be planned for
now, not retrofitted later.

Success looks like: a recruiter forms a confident impression in one screen; an
engineer reads a project or post to the end and comes away thinking the craft is real.

## Brand Personality

Precise, understated, crafted. Quiet confidence that lets the work speak — editorial
restraint paired with high-polish, deliberate detail. Every element is intentional;
nothing is loud for its own sake.

- **Voice/tone:** plain, exact, low-ego. No marketing gloss, no hype adjectives.
- **Emotional target:** calm and refined. The visitor should feel they're in capable,
  unhurried hands.
- **Note on current direction:** the existing Orbitron sci-fi monogram reads "sharp &
  technical." The redesign should move toward calm refinement — keep the precision and
  intentionality, shed any sci-fi loudness.

## Anti-references

This should NOT look like:

- **Generic SaaS / AI-portfolio template** — cream backgrounds, tiny tracked uppercase
  eyebrows over every section, identical icon-heading-text card grids, hero-metric
  blocks.
- **Cluttered / maximalist** — dense, busy layouts with many competing elements.
- **Corporate / sterile** — a faceless company site with no point of view.
- **Over-animated / gimmicky** — scroll-jacking, gratuitous motion, effects for their
  own sake. Motion, when present, is quiet and purposeful.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the work speak.** Content and craft carry the impression; chrome stays out of
   the way. Restraint is the default, emphasis the exception.
2. **Serve two reading speeds.** Fast credibility on the surface for skimmers, real
   depth underneath for readers — without making either pay for the other.
3. **Every element earns its place.** Intentional over decorative. If a thing doesn't
   serve clarity or character, it's cut.
4. **Refined, not flashy.** Polish shows in spacing, type, and timing — not in loud
   color or motion. Calm is a feature.
5. **Built for the long read.** Treat long-form (the blog) as a primary case from the
   start: comfortable measure, clear hierarchy, restful typography.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **Target: WCAG 2.2 AA.** Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast (large text ≥ 3:1), full keyboard
  navigability, visible focus states, semantic structure.
- **Motion:** every animation needs a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` alternative
  (crossfade or instant). This aligns with the "no gimmicky motion" stance — go beyond
  the minimum here.
- Support light and dark themes (already present) with contrast verified in both.
